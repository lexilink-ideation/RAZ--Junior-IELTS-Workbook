Vocabulary

🌉bridge(n.)
桥梁▾
Simple meaning
a structure built over water, a road, or a valley so people or vehicles can cross
Example
Let's look at a bridge being built over a river.
🔩steel(n.)
钢材▾
Simple meaning
a very strong metal used to build things like bridges and buildings
Example
Today, most bridges are made of steel and concrete.
🧱concrete(n.)
混凝土▾
Simple meaning
a hard building material made from cement, sand, water, and small stones
Example
The tubes, called piles, are filled with concrete.
🏛️pier(n.)
桥墩▾
Simple meaning
a strong post that holds up a bridge, like a leg holding up a table
Example
Next, workers build piers. They are like the legs on a table.
🪜girder(n.)
大梁▾
Simple meaning
a strong beam, usually made of concrete or steel, that holds up a roadway
Example
Concrete pieces, called girders, are put between two piers.
🚧dam(n.)
水坝▾
Simple meaning
a wall built to hold back water so an area can stay dry
Example
A dam holds the water back.
🛣️overpass(n.)
天桥▾
Simple meaning
a bridge that goes over a road or railroad
Example
An overpass is a bridge over a road or railroad.
💧aqueduct(n.)
渡槽▾
Simple meaning
a bridge built to carry water across land
Example
An aqueduct is a bridge over land used to carry water.
🚝viaduct(n.)
高架桥▾
Simple meaning
a low bridge that crosses dry land or a long valley
Example
A viaduct is a low bridge over dry land or a long valley.
🏞️causeway(n.)
堤道▾
Simple meaning
a long, low bridge that crosses a swamp or shallow body of water
Example
A causeway is a long, low bridge over a swamp or shallow body of water.

Story

Read the story. Target words are highlighted. 多读几次！
🌉 Title page photo showing a completed bridge, with the Focus Question printed beside it
Page 2
Focus Question: What helps make bridges strong?
🏜️ The Glen Canyon Dam Bridge in Arizona, high above the water, and the Golden Gate Bridge in San Francisco, close to the water
Pages 3-4
Bridges are long and short. They can be high above the water. They can be near the water. Bridges go over rivers and lakes. They go over bays and swamps. They go over highways and railroads. Some even float. They can carry cars, trucks, trains, and buses. Some carry people. Some carry water or oil.
🌏 The Sydney Harbor Bridge in Australia, and a simple rope bridge stretched across a river
Pages 5-6
The first bridges were made out of wood, rope, or stone. Today, most bridges are made of steel and concrete. The first bridges were simple. Some were just logs placed across a stream. Some were ropes stretched across a river.
💧 The Pont du Gard Aqueduct in France, and a table naming four bridge types: overpass, aqueduct, viaduct, and causeway
Pages 7-8
There are names for different types of bridges. This table shows some of the types. Overpass: a bridge over a road or railroad. Aqueduct: a bridge over land used to carry water. Viaduct: a low bridge over dry land or a long valley. Causeway: a long, low bridge over a swamp or shallow body of water. Later, stones were used to build stronger and longer bridges. In time, people were building bridges like the ones we see today. These are made of concrete and steel. They go across longer distances.
🏗️ The Clark Bridge over the Mississippi River at Alton, Illinois, and steel tubes being hammered into the river bottom
Pages 9-10
Building bridges is a big job. It can take many years. It can cost millions of dollars. It can take many workers to build a bridge. Let's look at a bridge being built over a river. First, workers must build supports under the water. They hammer large steel tubes into the river bottom. The tubes, called piles, are filled with concrete.
🚧 A diagram showing piers, a dam, and piles being installed underwater, and a photo of the dry work area made by a dam
Pages 11-12
Next, workers put supports across the piles. The supports are built under water using a special dam. A dam holds the water back. The workers can work where it is dry. Next, workers build piers. They are like the legs on a table. They will hold up the bridge.
🧱 Workers putting caps into place on top of the piers, and workers pouring concrete over steel bars on the roadway
Pages 13-14
Then caps are put on the tops of the piers. Concrete pieces, called girders, are put between two piers. The girders hold up the roadway. Steel bars are placed between the girders. The steel bars make the concrete stronger. Concrete is poured over the steel bars. The concrete gets hard. Finally, the new bridge is ready for traffic.

🧩 Sentence Patterns

First, ... Next, ... Finally, ...
A ___ is a bridge over ___.
They ___ to build the bridge.

📐 Key Grammar

Passive voice for processes: "Concrete is poured over the steel bars."
Sequencing words: First, workers build supports. Next, they build piers. Finally, the bridge is ready.
Modal "can" for ability/possibility: "Bridges can carry cars, trucks, trains, and buses."

💭 Things to Remember

Bridges come in many types and carry many things — cars, trains, people, even water or oil.
Modern bridges use steel and concrete, built with piles, piers, and girders for strength.
Building a bridge is a big project — it takes many workers, years, and millions of dollars.
