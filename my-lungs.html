Vocabulary

Tap each card to learn the word! 点击单词卡查看意思！
🫁lungs(n.)
肺▾
Simple meaning
parts of the body in the chest that are used for breathing
Example
My lungs are part of a system that keeps me alive.
💨breathe(v.)
呼吸▾
Simple meaning
to take in and let out air through the nose or mouth
Example
Oxygen comes from the air I breathe.
🌬️oxygen(n.)
氧气▾
Simple meaning
a gas that has no color, taste, or smell that most animals need to live
Example
My lungs pull oxygen from the air with each breath.
⚙️system(n.)
系统▾
Simple meaning
a group of things or ideas that work together
Example
My lungs are part of a system that keeps me alive.
👄throat(n.)
喉咙▾
Simple meaning
the inside of the neck where air travels to the lungs and food and drink pass to the stomach
Example
The air moves down my throat into my two lungs.
🍇sacs(n.)
囊袋▾
Simple meaning
body parts inside living things shaped like pouches, often holding air or fluid
Example
The tubes end in tiny sacs that look like bunches of grapes.
☁️carbon dioxide(n.)
二氧化碳▾
Simple meaning
a waste gas that the body does not need and breathes out
Example
My lungs trade oxygen for carbon dioxide through tiny balloons.
🌳branch(v.)
分支▾
Simple meaning
to split off into smaller parts, like the branches of a tree
Example
It moves through tubes in my lungs that branch like trees.
🔄trade(v.)
交换▾
Simple meaning
to give one thing and get another thing in return
Example
This trade happens with each breath.
🩸blood(n.)
血液▾
Simple meaning
the red liquid that moves through the body and carries oxygen
Example
My blood drops off the carbon dioxide in the tiny balloons.

🔄 A diagram of oxygen and carbon dioxide arrows swapping places inside a tiny balloon-shaped sac

My lungs trade oxygen for carbon dioxide through tiny balloons.
🩸 A diagram of blood carrying carbon dioxide to the lungs and picking up fresh oxygen to carry away
Page 9-10
Carbon dioxide is waste air. My body doesn't need carbon dioxide. My body sends carbon dioxide to my lungs in my blood. My blood drops off the carbon dioxide in the tiny balloons. Then, my blood picks up the oxygen and moves it through my body. This trade happens with each breath.
🫁 A side-by-side diagram of the chest expanding as a child breathes in and shrinking as the child breathes out
Page 11-12
As I breathe in, my chest moves out. My body makes room for my lungs to fill with oxygen. As I breathe out, my chest moves back in. The carbon dioxide moves out of my lungs.
📊 A bar graph showing 24 breaths per minute and 1,400 breaths per hour
Page 13-14
I breathe all the time, but I don't think about it. I don't have to think about it. If I did have to think about it, I'd think of nothing else. I take about 24 breaths each minute when I'm resting. That's more than 1,400 breaths each hour.
😊 A happy child taking a deep breath outdoors, smiling and thankful
Page 16

Reading Comprehension

Answer the questions about the story.

A · True or False 判断对错

1.The lungs help the body get the oxygen it needs.
2.Air moves into the lungs first through the ears.
3.The tubes in the lungs branch like trees and end in tiny sacs.
4.The body needs to keep carbon dioxide instead of sending it away.
5.A resting person takes about 24 breaths every minute.

B · Multiple Choice 选择题

1.Where does each breath come into the body first?
2.What warms up and cleans the air before it reaches the lungs?
3.What do the tiny sacs in the lungs look like?
4.What does the blood do in the tiny balloons (sacs)?
5.What happens to the chest when a person breathes in?

C · Short Answer 简答题

1.How do the lungs help the body get oxygen? Use details from the book.

Hint: Air comes in through the nose or mouth, moves down the throat, and into tubes that branch and end in tiny sacs where oxygen enters the blood.
2.What is the "trade" that happens in the tiny sacs with each breath?

Hint: The blood drops off carbon dioxide (waste) in the sacs and picks up oxygen to carry through the body.
3.Do you ever think about your breathing? When do you notice it the most?

📐 Key Grammar

Simple present tense for facts: "My lungs pull oxygen from the air."
Time clauses with "as": "As I breathe in, my chest moves out."
Relative clauses with "that": "tubes in my lungs that branch like trees."

💭 Things to Remember

Air enters through the nose or mouth, gets warmed and cleaned, then travels down the throat.
Tiny sacs in the lungs trade oxygen for carbon dioxide with the blood.
A resting person breathes about 24 times a minute — more than 1,400 times an hour!
