Story

Read the story. Target words are highlighted. 多读几次！
👧🐕 Young Jane with her dog Rusty, reading about animals
Page 4
The Watcher: As a young girl, Jane Goodall loved to watch animals. She read about them, too. She dreamed of living in Africa one day. She hoped to write about the wild animals there. Jane Goodall was born in England in 1934. At that time, most young girls grew up to live quiet lives at home. Jane loved to play with her pets, climb trees, and read. She wanted a life of travel and adventure.
🧑‍🔬 Goodall meeting a famous scientist in Africa
Page 5–6
Off to Africa: When she was old enough, Goodall worked and saved her money. When she had saved enough, she went on a trip to Africa. In Africa, Goodall met a famous scientist. The scientist needed help with a new study about chimpanzees. The study would look at how they behave. He asked Goodall to work on the study with him.
🌳🦍 Goodall watching chimps from far away in the Gombe Forest
Page 7–8
Into the Forest: To work on the study, Goodall moved to the Gombe (GOME-bay) Forest. She watched the chimps every day. The Gombe Gang: At first, most of the chimps were afraid of Goodall. Only a chimp she named David trusted her. He let Goodall meet his family.
🦍🌾 A chimp using a long blade of grass to catch termites
Page 9–10
Goodall said chimps were smart. She said they used sticks as tools to dig up bugs to eat. She also said chimps showed feelings, as humans do. Goodall took notes about the chimps. Her notes upset many scientists. They had their own ideas about how chimps behaved.
😊😢😠 Chimps that look happy, sad and angry
Page 11–12
Goodall’s work made some scientists think again about chimps. They saw that some of their old ideas might be wrong. Goodall saw chimps that were happy, sad, and angry. She saw that each chimp behaved in its own way, just as humans do.
🪓🌲 People cutting down trees in the forest; Goodall speaking to a crowd
Page 13–14
Trouble in the Forest: After many years in Gombe, Goodall became worried. Many people in this part of Africa are very poor. Some were cutting down the forest for firewood or to grow crops there. Some were stealing baby chimps and selling them. Goodall knew what she had to do. She went around the world, talking about her work. She asked for help to save the chimps and the forest.
🌍💚 Goodall visiting a chimp rescue center today
Page 15–16
Jane Goodall Today: People trusted Goodall. They helped to support her work in Gombe. Today, she still works to protect the forest and the chimpanzees.
